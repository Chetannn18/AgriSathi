# Rice Blast Disease Diagnosis and Control Guide

## Overview & Symptoms
Rice blast (*Magnaporthe oryzae*) affects paddy at all growth stages from seedlings in nursery to panicle development. High nitrogen fertilizer application, cloudy skies, high relative humidity (>85%), and cool night temperatures (18°C–22°C) favor rapid spread.

Visible symptoms:
- Diamond or spindle-shaped lesions with gray or whitish centers and dark reddish-brown margins on leaf blades.
- Coalescence of spots leading to entire leaf blighting and seedling death ("leaf blast").
- Blackened, rotted neck node of the panicle ("neck blast"), causing blank or partially filled grains that break and fall.

## Immediate Practical Actions
1. **Regulate Nitrogen:** Immediately stop top-dressing urea or high nitrogen fertilizers if blast lesions appear. Excess nitrogen creates succulent leaves vulnerable to fungal penetration.
2. **Water Management:** Maintain 2–3 cm standing water in paddy fields; avoid drought stress as dry soil conditions exacerbate blast susceptibility.
3. **Silicon Amendment:** Apply potassium silicate or rice husk ash to strengthen cell wall silicon barriers against hyphal penetration.

## Field Interventions
- Spray Tricyclazole 75% WP (0.6 g/L) or Isoprothiolane 40% EC (1.5 ml/L) at boot leaf or early heading stage.
- Biological: Apply *Bacillus subtilis* or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* seed treatment (10 g/kg seed) for nursery protection.
