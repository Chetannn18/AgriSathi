# Management of Early Blight in Tomato (Alternaria solani)

## Overview & Symptoms
Early blight, caused by the fungal pathogen *Alternaria solani*, is one of the most prevalent diseases affecting tomato cultivation across India, especially during warm and humid periods (temperatures of 24°C–29°C with relative humidity above 80%).

Visible symptoms include:
- Circular brown to black spots with concentric rings ("target board" appearance) on older lower leaves first.
- Surrounding tissue turns chlorotic (yellow) as lesions enlarge.
- Premature defoliation, leaving developing fruits vulnerable to sunscald.
- Dark, sunken, leathery cankers on stems and fruit calyx.

## Contributing Environmental Conditions
- Frequent rainfall followed by warm sunny spells.
- Overhead sprinkler irrigation splashing fungal spores from the soil onto lower foliage.
- Dense plant canopy with inadequate air circulation.

## Immediate Practical Actions for Farmers
1. **Sanitation:** Immediately hand-prune lower infected leaves showing target-board lesions. Dispose of or bury them away from the field; do not leave infected debris on mulch.
2. **Watering Practice:** Switch to furrow or drip irrigation to keep leaf canopies dry. Avoid evening or overhead sprinkler irrigation.
3. **Staking and Mulching:** Stake tomato plants to keep foliage elevated off soil moisture. Apply organic straw or plastic mulch to prevent rain-splash transmission.

## Integrated Disease Management & Safe Interventions
- **Biological / Organic:** Foliar spray of *Trichoderma harzianum* or *Pseudomonas fluorescens* (5-10 g/liter of water) at vegetative initiation. Neem oil (0.5% concentration with soap emulsifier) suppresses early spore germination.
- **Approved Fungicidal Intervention:** If infection exceeds 15% leaf area under high humidity, spray Mancozeb 75% WP (2 g/L) or Copper Oxychloride 50% WP (2.5 g/L). Alternate with systemic fungicides like Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole as per state KVK recommendations. Always adhere strictly to harvest waiting periods (PHI).

## Monitoring & Follow-up
Inspect the underside of lower leaves every 3–4 days after rain events. If lesions cease expanding and margins dry up, the disease is stabilized.
