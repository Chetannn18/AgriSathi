# Management of Powdery Mildew in Soybean (Erysiphe diffusa)

## Symptoms and Identification
Powdery Mildew caused by *Erysiphe diffusa* manifests as:
- **White Powdery Patches:** Distinctive talcum-powder like white to grayish fungal patches scattered over the upper leaf surfaces.
- **Chlorosis & Senescence:** Severely affected leaves turn pale yellow, dry up, curl downwards, and may drop prematurely.
- **Stems and Pods:** In heavy outbreaks, white fungal growth can extend to petioles, stems, and developing pods, drastically reducing photosynthetic capacity and test weight.
- **Favorable Weather:** Cool night temperatures combined with dry, warm sunny days and moderate humidity favor rapid conidial proliferation.

## Immediate Control Actions
1. **Sulfur Fungicide:** Spray Wettable Sulphur 80% WP @ 3.0 g/L (or 1.25 kg/ha). Sulfur provides rapid eradication of powdery mildew.
2. **Systemic Fungicides:** In established infections, spray Hexaconazole 5% EC @ 1.0 ml/L OR Tebuconazole 25.9% EC @ 1.0 ml/L OR Difenoconazole 25% EC @ 0.5 ml/L.
3. **Pest Control:** Check for sap-sucking pests (whitefly, aphids, jassids) or small caterpillars that often accompany foliar stresses and spread spores.

## Preventive Cultural Practices
- Avoid dense plant populations; maintain recommended 45 x 10 cm row-to-plant spacing to facilitate air circulation.
- Balanced nutrition: Avoid excessive nitrogenous fertilizers which promote succulent foliage vulnerable to powdery mildew. Ensure adequate potassium.
