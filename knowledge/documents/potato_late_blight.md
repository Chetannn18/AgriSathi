# Late Blight of Potato Management and Emergency Advisory

## Overview & Symptoms
Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*) is an aggressive oomycete disease capable of destroying entire potato fields within 7 to 10 days if unchecked during cool, overcast, and high-humidity weather (temperatures 10°C–20°C, RH > 90%).

Visible symptoms:
- Water-soaked irregular pale green lesions on leaf tips or margins rapidly turning dark brown to black.
- Delicate white downy fungal-like growth visible on the undersides of leaves, especially in early morning dampness.
- Dark necrotic lesions spreading along stems and petioles causing stem collapse.
- Tubers develop firm, dry, copper-brown to purplish rot beneath the skin.

## Immediate Practical Actions for Farmers
1. **Field Inspection:** Check low-lying damp areas of the field first where morning dew lingers.
2. **Cease Overhead Watering:** Suspend sprinkler irrigation immediately. Ensure drainage furrows are clear of standing water.
3. **Dehaulming:** In cases of severe foliage infection nearing harvest, cut and remove the haulms (green foliage) 10-12 days before digging tubers to protect harvested potatoes from spore wash.

## Recommended Management & Safeguards
- **Prophylactic Spray:** Before disease appearance when weather forecast predicts continuous fog or rain, spray Mancozeb 75% WP (2.5 g/L) or Chlorothalonil 75% WP (2 g/L).
- **Curative Treatment:** Upon early onset, apply Cymoxanil 8% + Mancozeb 64% WP (3 g/L) or Dimethomorph 50% WP (1 g/L). Ensure complete coverage of lower canopy.
- Note: Always use protective gear when spraying and follow approved state agricultural university dosages.
